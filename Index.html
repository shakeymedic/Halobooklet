How to use this guide
1. The Two-Page System
Every procedure consists of two sequential pages:

Page A: The Walkthrough (Preparation)
Use this during the "Kit Dump" phase. It includes specific equipment checklists and detailed setup instructions.

Page B: The Flowchart (Action)
Use this during the procedure itself. Designed for high-stress cognitive processing.
2. Design Principles
Standard Steps (Grey): Routine actions and setups.
Critical Steps (Red): Dangerous actions (incisions) or stops.
Plan B (Red Box): Fallback plans if the primary method fails.
3. Human Factors & Safety
Allocate a Reader: Hand this book to a specific team member. Instruct them to read the steps aloud to reduce cognitive load for the operator.
Pre-procedure Pause: Pause for 5 seconds before making any incision. Verbally confirm equipment, landmarks, and check the patient for severe allergies.

Resuscitative Hysterotomy

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Call for Help & Roles
• Call 2222 "Maternal Cardiac Arrest".
• Declare Major Haemorrhage Protocol.
• Assign Roles:
1. Person to manage Major Haemorrhage (blood/transfusion).
2. Person to lead ALS (Defib/Drugs).
3. Allocate a dedicated team for Neonatal Resuscitation.
• Start ALS + Obstetric Modifications:
• Manually displace uterus to patient's LEFT.
• Intubate early (smaller ETT e.g., 6.0/7.0).
• IV access x2 (Above Diaphragm).
Equipment & Setup
Chlorhexidine (Splash)
Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Scalpel: Size 10 or 20
Tuff Cut Scissors
Suction x2 (Wide bore/Yankauer)
Large Swabs (for packing)
Stapler / Clingfilm / Large Dressings
Post-Procedure Plan
If ROSC Achieved:
• Mass closure of abdomen with large dressings, clingfilm or staples.
• Immediate Transfer: Emergent Theatre with Obstetrics.
• Continue ALS / Stabilisation during transfer.
Procedure Walkthrough
  1. Clean: Rapid splash with CHLORHEXIDINE.
  2. Skin Incision: Vertical incision from Xiphisternum to Pubic Symphysis. Cut deep through skin and fat with SCALPEL.
  3. Peritoneum: Bluntly enter peritoneum with fingers (tear it open) to avoid bowel injury.
  4. Uterus Exposure: Retract abdominal wall laterally.
  5. Uterine Incision: Vertical incision in the upper segment of uterus. Use SCALPEL to start, then TUFF CUT SCISSORS.
  6. Protect Baby: Insert two fingers to lift uterine wall away from fetal head. Extend cut with TUFF CUT SCISSORS.
  7. Delivery: Scoop head and shoulders. Do not pull by a single limb. Rotate if necessary.
  8. Placenta: One gentle tug with smooth traction. If not easy, leave in situ. Pack uterus with SWABS.
Vertical Midline Incision: Speed is priority

Resuscitative Hysterotomy

Page 2: Action (Perimortem C-Section)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
  • GOAL: DELIVERY in less than 5 MINS (Timer starts at the exact moment of maternal cardiac arrest).
  • Indication: Maternal Arrest. Uterus greater than or equal to Umbilicus.
  • Call: 2222 Maternal Cardiac Arrest.
1
ABDOMINAL INCISION
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • Vertical Midline: Xiphisternum to Pubic Symphysis.
  • Incise deep through skin/fat. Separate rectus muscles bluntly.
  • Ignore bleeding; there is no cardiac output.
2
UTERINE INCISION
  • Enter peritoneum bluntly with fingers.
  • Vertical Incision in uterus (Avoids bladder/arteries).
  • Insert fingers to protect baby's face. Extend with TUFF CUT SCISSORS.
3
DELIVERY
  • Scoop head and shoulders. Do not pull on a single limb.
  • Rotate if necessary to free shoulders.
  • Clamp and Cut Cord. Handover to NLS Team.
4
POST-DELIVERY & CLOSURE
  • Placenta: One gentle tug. If stuck, leave in situ. One hand sweep if bleeding.
  • Check allergies. Drugs: Oxytocin 10iu IV, Antibiotics, TXA 1g. Fetch Drugs
  • Closure: Pack uterus. Close abdomen with dressings, clingfilm or staples.
  • Transfer: Emergent Theatre (Obstetrics) if ROSC.

Emergency Breech

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

Call & Principles
• Call 2222 "Obstetric Emergency".
• Position: Lithotomy. Buttocks to edge of bed.
• Team: Obstetric Reg, Midwife, Neonatal Team, Anaesthetics.
• HANDS OFF: Do NOT touch baby until umbilicus is visible.
• Gravity: Allow baby to hang. Avoid traction.
• Time: Deliver head within 5 mins of buttocks.
Specific Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Sterile Drape
Neonatal Resuscitation Kit
Oxytocin (for post-delivery) Fetch Drug
GTN Spray (Uterine relaxation) Fetch Drug
Episiotomy Scissors
Resuscitaire (Heater On)
Post-Procedure Plan
• Inspect for perineal tears.
• Paediatric review for baby.
• Transfer: Obstetric Unit / Theatre for repair.
Manoeuvres (If descent stops)
  1. Legs Stuck (Pinard's):
    • Insert fingers behind fetal knee (popliteal fossa).
    • Press to abduct thigh (move away from midline).
    • Leg will flex. Sweep it out.
  2. Arms Stuck (Loveset's):
    • Hold bony pelvis (thumbs on sacrum).
    • Rotate baby 180 degrees (Keep back up/anterior).
    • Posterior arm sweeps down over face. Deliver arm.
    • Rotate back 180 degrees to deliver second arm.
  3. Head Stuck (Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit):
    • Assistant: Suprapubic pressure (flexes head).
    • Operator: Rest baby on forearm ("Flying horse").
    • Fingers on cheekbones (malar). Flex head. Pull gently.
Pinard's: Abduct thigh to flex knee

Emergency Breech

Page 2: Action (Vaginal Manoeuvres)

  • THE GOLDEN RULE: HANDS OFF.
  • Allow breech to hang by gravity.
  • Keep room warm. Note time of buttock delivery.
1
LEGS STUCK (Pinard's)
  • Indication: Frank Breech (legs extended).
  • Finger in popliteal fossa.
  • Abduct thigh laterally. Knee flexes. Sweep out.
2
ARMS STUCK (Loveset's)
  • Sign: Winged Scapula visible.
  • Hold bony pelvis. Rotate 180 degrees. Arm sweeps over chest.
  • Rotate back 180 degrees for second arm.
3
HEAD STUCK (MSV)
  • Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit:
  • Check for umbilical cord around neck. Slip it over the head if present.
  • Baby on forearm. Fingers on cheekbones. Flex head.
  • Tip: Assistant applies Suprapubic Pressure.
4
POST-PROCEDURE
  • Inspect for perineal tears.
  • Paediatric review for baby (Check hips/limbs).
  • Active management of third stage (Oxytocin 10iu).

Reference: RCOG Green-top 20b. Open Guideline

TRAUMA ARREST

Resuscitative Thoracotomy

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Strict Indications
• Call 2222 "Trauma Cardiac Arrest". Activate Major Haemorrhage Protocol. Request O-negative blood.
• Penetrating: Arrest less than 15 mins.
• Blunt: Arrest less than 10 mins (Highly selective).
• Signs of Life: Pupils reacting, gasping, ECG activity, cardiac motion on US.
• Goal: Release Tamponade, Internal Massage, Clamp Aorta.
Equipment Tray
Chlorhexidine
Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Scalpel: Size 22 (Large blade)
Tuff Cut Scissors (Heavy Duty)
Gigli Saw (Handles + Wire) or Bone Cutters
Finochietto Retractor (Rib Spreader)
Toothed Forceps (Bonney's)
Vascular Clamp (Satinsky/DeBakey)
Internal Paddles (for Defib)
Artery Forceps (Spencer Wells/Kellys)
Foley Catheter (20F)
Prolene/Silk 2-0 (Horizontal Mattress)
Dressings (Large)
Post-Procedure Plan
If ROSC Achieved:
• Close the chest with large dressings.
• Immediate Transfer: Emergent Theatre with Cardiothoracics.
• Sedate and Paralyse immediately.
Clamshell Technique
  1. PREP: Splash CHLORHEXIDINE. Stop ventilation.
  2. INCISION (SKIN): 4th/5th ICS. SCALPEL from Mid-Axillary to Mid-Axillary.
  3. INCISION (DEEP): Use TUFF CUT SCISSORS to cut through muscle and intercostals.
  4. STERNUM: Tunnel TOOTHED FORCEPS under sternum. Cut sternum with GIGLI SAW or heavy shears.
  5. RETRACT: Insert FINOCHIETTO RETRACTOR. Handle points DOWN. Crank open.
  6. PERICARDIUM: Lift with TOOTHED FORCEPS. Cut longitudinally (Head to Toe).
  7. DELIVER HEART: Scoop heart out of sac. Inspect for wounds.
  8. CONTROL: Internal massage. Place VASCULAR CLAMP on Aorta. Suture wounds.
Clamshell: Mid-axillary to Mid-axillary

Resuscitative Thoracotomy

Page 2: Action ("The Clamshell")

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
  • Penetrating: Arrest less than 15 mins + Signs of Life.
  • Blunt: Arrest less than 10 mins + Witnessed.
  • Call: 2222 Trauma Cardiac Arrest. Activate Major Haemorrhage Protocol.
1
INCISION ORDER
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • 1. Scalpel: Cut Skin and Subcut fat (Mid-Ax to Mid-Ax).
  • 2. Tuff Cut Scissors: Cut Muscle and Sternum.
  • Stop ventilation before cutting pleura.
2
ACTIONS (H.O.T.)
  • Heart: Relieve tamponade. Open Pericardium (Longitudinal).
  • Outflow: Plug holes (Foley/Finger).
  • Thorax: Clamp Descending Aorta against spine.
3
REPAIR (SUTURING)
  • Use Horizontal Mattress Sutures (Prolene/Silk).
  • Use Pledgets if available (to prevent tear through).
  • ⚠️ WARNING: DO NOT OCCLUDE CORONARY ARTERIES (LAD/CIRCUMFLEX). Lift vessels to suture under them if needed.
4
ROSC, CLOSURE & DEFIBRILLATION
  • Internal Defib: Sterile paddles directly on heart. 10 to 20 Joules.
  • No Internal Paddles?
    • Remove Finochietto Retractor.
    • Close Chest manually (push ribs together).
    • Defib externally (200J). Ensure no one touching.
  • ROSC? Clamp bleeding Internal Mammary Arteries with artery forceps.
🛑 STOP CRITERIA
Asystole > 20 mins despite Tamponade release, Aortic Clamp and Fluid loading.
Consensus decision to cease.

Reference: FPHC and RCEM Consensus Statement (2018/2025). Open Guideline

Escharotomy

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Indications & Criteria
• Call 2222 "Trauma Team" or "Anaesthetics".
• Circumferential full thickness burns (Painless, leathery, white/charred).
• Chest: Ventilation difficulty. High peak pressures (greater than 30). Hypoxia.
• Limbs: Ischaemia. Pain on passive stretch. Loss of pulses (Late sign).
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Scalpel (Size 22)
Sharpie Marker (Draw lines first)
Haemostatic Gauze
Artery Forceps
Adrenaline 1:1000 (Soaked gauze) Fetch Drug
Loose Bandages (Crepe)
Mark lines before cutting
Incision Technique
  1. MARK: Draw incision lines with MARKER. Follow anatomical safe lines.
  2. DEPTH: Cut through eschar (burned skin) down to subcutaneous fat with SCALPEL.
    STOP: Do not cut deep fascia or muscle.
  3. RELEASE: Edges must gape widely. Run finger along cut to ensure no tight bands remain.
    Warning: Releasing eschar causes underlying vessels to bleed profusely. Prepare ARTERY FORCEPS to clamp large vessels. Pack with HAEMOSTATIC GAUZE.
  4. CHEST ("Roman Breastplate"):
    • Vertical: Bilateral Anterior Axillary Lines.
    • Horizontal: Connect Clavicles and Costal Margins.
  5. UPPER LIMB:
    • Mid-Lateral and Mid-Medial lines (Shoulder to wrist).
    • Elbow Safety: Pass ANTERIOR to medial epicondyle (Avoid Ulnar Nerve).
  6. LOWER LIMB:
    • Mid-Lateral and Mid-Medial lines (Hip to ankle).
    • Knee Safety: Pass POSTERIOR to fibular head (Avoid Common Peroneal Nerve).
Post-Procedure Plan
• Check distal perfusion with Doppler probe.
• Apply haemostatic gauze. Loosely bandage.
• Elevate limbs.
• Transfer: Specialist Burns Centre / ICU.

Escharotomy

Page 2: Action (Burns Release)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
  • Call: 2222 Trauma / Medical Emergency.
  • Chest: Ventilatory compromise.
  • Limbs: Ischaemia (Pallor, Absent Doppler).
  • Procedure is painless in full-thickness burns.
1
CHEST ("BREASTPLATE")
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • Vertical: Bilateral Anterior Axillary Lines.
  • Transverse: Connect tops (Clavicle) and bottoms (Costal Margin).
  • Result: Chest wall must spring open.
2
UPPER LIMBS
  • Lines: Mid-Lateral and Mid-Medial.
  • Hand: Dorsal incisions over metacarpals.
  • Ulnar Nerve: Cut ANTERIOR to Medial Epicondyle.
3
LOWER LIMBS
  • Lines: Mid-Lateral and Mid-Medial.
  • Peroneal Nerve: Cut POSTERIOR to Fibular Head.
4
POST-PROCEDURE
  • Check distal perfusion with Doppler probe. If pulses remain absent, extend incisions deeper into subcutaneous fat.
  • Prepare to clamp large bleeding vessels.
  • Apply haemostatic gauze. Loosely bandage.
  • Elevate limbs.
  • Transfer: Specialist Burns Centre / ICU.

Reference: LSEBN Guidelines (2025). Open Guideline

Chest Drain (Open)

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Indications
• Call 2222 "Trauma" or "Medical Emergency".
• Trauma: Haemothorax, Pneumothorax (ventilated/unstable).
• Medical: Empyema, Massive Effusion.
• Tension Pneumothorax: Decompress with needle/finger FIRST if arresting.
• Contraindications: Coagulopathy (Relative), Diaphragmatic Hernia.
Equipment
Chlorhexidine + Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Lidocaine 1% (20ml) Fetch Drug
Scalpel (Size 10)
Spencer Wells Forceps (Straight)
Curved Kelly Forceps (Alternative)
Chest Tube: 28-32F (Trauma), 12-14F (Air)
Silk Suture (1-0)
Underwater Seal (Primed)
Dressings (Tegaderm/Mepore)
Triangle of Safety: Lat Dorsi, Pec Major, Axilla, Nipple
Open Technique
  1. POSITION: Supine. Arm abducted behind head. Secure arm.
  2. LANDMARK: Safety Triangle. 5th Intercostal Space, Anterior Axillary Line.
  3. LOCAL: Check allergies. Infiltrate skin, muscle, and pleura. Aspirate fluid/air to confirm depth.
  4. INCISION: 3 to 4cm Transverse cut parallel to ribs with SCALPEL.
  5. DISSECT: Use Straight SPENCER WELLS FORCEPS. Push closed, open to spread. Tunnel over the UPPER BORDER of the rib below (Avoid intercostal vessels).
  6. BREACH: Push forceps firmly through pleura ("POP"). Open jaws wide to dilate track.
  7. SWEEP: Insert finger. Rotate 360 degrees. Feel lung. Break adhesions. Confirm you are in chest, not abdomen.
  8. INSERT: Clamp CHEST TUBE tip with forceps. Guide into chest. Aim Apical (Air) or Basal (Blood). Connect seal. Secure with SILK SUTURE.
Post-Procedure Plan
• Connect to underwater seal.
• Secure with sutures/dressings.
• Request CXR to confirm position.
• Monitor output.

Chest Drain (Open)

Page 2: Action (Trauma / Haemothorax)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
1
SAFETY TRIANGLE
  • Lat Dorsi (Posterior). Pec Major (Anterior). Axilla (Superior).
  • Target: 5th ICS, Anterior Axillary Line.
  • Call: 2222 Medical Emergency.
2
DISSECTION
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • Check allergies. Give Local Anaesthetic. 3cm Transverse incision.
  • Blunt dissection with Spencer Wells Forceps.
  • Stay on Upper Border of Lower Rib (avoid vessels).
3
PLEURAL ENTRY
  • Breach pleura with forceps ("Pop"). Dilate track.
  • Finger Sweep mandatory (Verify cavity).
  • Insert Tube. Look for fogging/swinging.
🛑 PLAN B: CANNOT INSERT?
• Enlarge incision. Insert Finger again.
• Use Bougie to guide tube.
• Decompress with Finger Thoracostomy if arresting.
4
SECURING & POST-PROCEDURE
  • Connect to underwater seal.
  • Suture: Mattress or Stay suture (1-0 Silk). Tape securely.
  • Post-Procedure: Request CXR to confirm position. Monitor drain output.

Pericardiocentesis

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Indications
• Call 2222 "Cardiac Arrest" or "Medical Emergency".
• Non-Traumatic Tamponade: Viral, Malignancy, Uraemia.
• Signs (Beck's): Hypotension, Distended Neck Veins, Muffled Sounds.
• POCUS: Fluid + RV Diastolic Collapse.
• Trauma Warning: Blood clots rapidly. Thoracotomy is superior.
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Ultrasound (Probe Cover + Gel)
Pericardiocentesis Kit OR
Central Line Kit (16G Grey Cannula)
20ml Syringe
3-Way Tap (Essential)
Agitated Saline (for verification) Fetch Saline
Dressings
Agitated Saline (Verification)
1. Make: 9ml Saline + 1ml Air. Agitate between two SYRINGES via 3-WAY TAP until frothy.
2. Deliver: Inject rapidly via drain/needle while assistant watches ULTRASOUND.
3. Look for: Bubbles (Hyperechoic swirl) appearing in the pericardial fluid.
Echo-Guided Approach
Strategy: Find the largest pocket closest to skin (less than 1cm) using ULTRASOUND.
  1. Apical (Preferred):
    • 1 to 2cm Lateral to Apex Beat.
    • Angle at 45 degrees.
    • Aim towards Right Shoulder. Glide over rib.
    • Often safest route (avoids liver).
  2. Sub-Xiphoid (Traditional):
    • 1cm inferior to Left Xiphocostal angle.
    • Shallow angle (15 degrees). Aim towards Left Shoulder.
    • Longer path, passes near liver.
  3. Parasternal:
    • 5th Left ICS, close to sternum.
    • Insert Perpendicular.
    • Risk: Internal Thoracic Artery.
Post-Procedure Plan
• Secure drain. CXR.
• Urgent Cardiology/CT Surgery referral.
Use Ultrasound to select best window

Pericardiocentesis

Page 2: Action (Tamponade Decompression)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
  • Beck's Triad (Hypotension, Muffled Sounds, JVP).
  • POCUS: RV Diastolic Collapse = Tamponade.
  • In Traumatic Arrest use Thoracotomy.
  • Call: 2222 Cardiac Arrest.
1
APPROACH
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • Apical: Shortest path. Good seal. Angle 45 degrees to Right Shoulder.
  • Sub-Xiphoid: Shallow angle. Aim Left Shoulder.
  • Parasternal: Perpendicular. Watch for vessels.
2
EQUIPMENT HACK
  • No Kit? Use Central Line 16G (Grey) Cannula.
  • Attach 3-Way Tap.
  • Advantage: Leave plastic cannula in as drain.
3
EXECUTION & POST-PROCEDURE
  • Advance under US guidance. Aspirate continuously.
  • Verification: Inject agitated saline (Bubbles in pericardium?).
  • Fluid should NOT clot (defibrinated). If blood clots in syringe, suspect Right Ventricle puncture.
  • After: Secure drain. CXR. Urgent Cardiology/CT Surgery referral.
Feedback/Updates: jake.turner@uhb.nhs.uk
Reference: ESC Guidelines (2015/2025). Open Guideline

External Pacing

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

Indications
• Call 2222 "Medical Emergency".
• Unstable Bradycardia: less than 40 bpm + Shock/Syncope/Ischaemia.
• Failed drug therapy (Atropine/Glyco).
• Risk of Asystole: Mobitz II, Complete Heart Block, Pause greater than 3s.
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Defibrillator (Pacer Mode)
Pacing Pads (AP Position)
3-Lead ECG (Required for sensing)
Sedation (Ketamine/Midazolam) Fetch Drug
Analgesia (Fentanyl) Fetch Drug
Post-Procedure Plan
• Cardiology Referral for temporary wire.
• Monitor conscious level (Sedation).
Step-by-Step Configuration
  1. PADS: Apply Anterior-Posterior (Sandwich the heart).
  2. LEADS: Connect 3-LEAD ECG. Confirm R-wave markers on screen.
  3. MODE: Select PACER. Mode: Demand.
  4. RATE: Set to 70 to 80 bpm.
  5. CURRENT (mA): Start 30 mA. Increase by 10 mA.
  6. ELECTRICAL CAPTURE: Pacer Spike -> Broad QRS -> T wave.
  7. MECHANICAL CAPTURE: Palpate Femoral Pulse. Measure blood pressure immediately after capture.
    WARNING: Skeletal muscle twitching is NOT capture. Carotid pulse is unreliable due to neck twitching.
  8. SETTING: Find threshold. Set final output to Threshold + 10 mA.
Anterior-Posterior Position is Best

External Pacing

Page 2: Action (Unstable Bradycardia)

  • Symptomatic Bradycardia unresponsive to drugs.
  • Sedation: Pacing is painful. Use Ketamine/Midaz.
  • Call: 2222 Medical Emergency.
1
SETUP
  • Pads: Anterior-Posterior preferred.
  • Connect 3-Lead ECG (Critical for sensing).
  • Select "Pacer". Rate: 70 bpm.
2
CAPTURE (mA)
  • Increase Current (mA) until capture seen.
  • Electrical Capture: Spike followed by Broad QRS.
  • Ignore skeletal muscle twitching. Look at ECG.
3
CONFIRMATION & POST-PROCEDURE
  • Mechanical Capture: Palpate Femoral Pulse. Measure BP immediately.
  • Ensure pulse matches pacer rate.
  • Set output 10 mA above threshold.
  • After: Refer to Cardiology for Transvenous/Permanent Pacemaker. Monitor conscious level.
🛑 PLAN B: NO CAPTURE?
• Check Pads/Leads. Swap Pad position.
• Start Adrenaline/Isoprenaline Infusion.
• Consider Hyperkalaemia/Toxicity.
Feedback/Updates: jake.turner@uhb.nhs.uk
Reference: Resus Council UK ALS 2021. Open Guideline

Subclavian Line

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Indications
• Call 2222 "Medical Emergency" or "Trauma".
• Hypovolaemic Shock (Vein remains patent).
• C-Spine Immobilisation (IJ inaccessible).
• Contraindications: Coagulopathy (Relative), Contralateral Pneumothorax.
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Central Line Kit (Triple Lumen)
Sterile Gown, Gloves, Drapes
Lidocaine 1% Fetch Drug
Saline Flushes x3 Fetch Saline
Suture (Silk) + Scalpel
Dressings (Tegaderm)
Post-Procedure Plan
• CXR Mandatory.
• Check line position.
• Secure well.
Landmark Technique
  1. POSITION: 15 degree Trendelenburg. Rolled towel vertically between shoulder blades. Retract shoulders down.
  2. LANDMARK: Junction of Medial 1/3 and Middle 1/3 of Clavicle.
  3. LOCAL: Check allergies. Infiltrate skin and periosteum with SYRINGE.
  4. INSERTION: 1cm inferior to clavicle junction. Bevel Up.
  5. AIM: Place finger in Sternal Notch. Aim towards it.
  6. TRAJECTORY: Keep needle PARALLEL TO FLOOR.
    • Hit clavicle.
    • "Walk" under the bone.
    • Aspirate constantly.
  7. ACCESS: Venous Flash -> Stabilise -> WIRE (Must flow freely) -> DILATOR -> CATHETER.
  8. CHECK: CXR Mandatory (Check for Pneumothorax and Tip Position).
Landmark: Medial/Mid 1/3 Clavicle -> Sternal Notch

Subclavian Line

Page 2: Action (Infraclavicular Approach)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
  • Use Landmark Technique.
  • Right side preferred (Lower pleural dome).
  • Call: 2222 Medical Emergency.
1
POSITION
  • Trendelenburg 15 degrees. Towel between scapulae.
  • Shoulders retracted down. Head turned away.
2
LANDMARKS
  • Pause for 5 seconds. Confirm landmarks.
  • Junction of Medial/Middle third of clavicle.
  • Insert 1cm below. Aim: Sternal Notch.
  • Keep flat: Parallel to floor (Avoids lung apex).
3
SELDINGER & POST-PROCEDURE
  • Flash -> Wire (must slide easily).
  • Dilate -> Catheter -> Aspirate all ports -> Suture.
  • Mandatory CXR. (Exclude Pneumothorax).
  • Check line position before use.

Lateral Canthotomy

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Indications
• Call 2222 Medical Emergency (if associated trauma).
• Retrobulbar Haematoma: Orbital Compartment Syndrome.
• Signs: Proptosis, Rock Hard Eye, APD, Vision Loss, 'Cherry Red' macula.
• IOP: greater than 40 mmHg.
• No Tonometer? Test by Palpation, Visual Acuity or Ocular US.
• Rule: Do NOT wait for CT. Time is Vision (less than 120 mins).
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Sterile Drape / Gown Pack
Lidocaine 1% or 2% with Adrenaline Fetch Drug
2ml Syringe + Orange Needle
Saline Irrigation
Suturing Kit (Scissors/Forceps)
Sterile Gauze
Post-Procedure Plan
• Urgent Ophthalmology Review.
• CT Orbit.
• Medical Management (Mannitol/Acetazolamide).
Procedure Steps
  1. PREP: Clean with SALINE IRRIGATION. Check allergies. Inject LA into lateral canthus (Aim away from globe).
  2. CRUSH: Clamp MOSQUITO FORCEPS horizontally across the lateral canthus (approx 1cm). Compress for 1 min (haemostasis).
  3. CANTHOTOMY (Skin): Remove forceps. Cut the crushed line horizontally to orbital rim with SCISSORS. Point SCISSORS laterally and away from the eye to protect the globe.
  4. CANTHOLYSIS (Inferior Crus):
    • Pull lower eyelid down and out.
    • Palpate the tight "guitar string" band (Inferior Crus).
    • "Strum" it with SCISSORS to identify.
    • CUT the Inferior Crus.
  5. CHECK: Lower lid should fall away. IOP should drop.
  6. STILL HIGH? Cut the Superior Crus (Upper lid band).

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

🧰 HALO TROLLEY
Priorities
• Call 2222 "Major Haemorrhage".
• Airway: Distortion, loose teeth, blood, swelling.
• Plan A: Conscious? Sit up + Suction.
• Plan B: RSI. Anticipate difficult view. Have eFONA ready.
• Bleeding: Maxillary artery is prolific. Scalp wounds bleed heavily.
• C-Spine: Maintain precautions.
Equipment
Chlorhexidine
Suction x2 (Wide bore)
Bite Blocks / McKesson Props
Option A: Nasal Epistats (Double Balloon)
Option B: TWO Foley Catheters (12-14F)
Rapid Rhino Packs (7.5cm / 9cm)
Heavy Suture (0 Silk) - Tongue Stitch
Dressings/Packs
TXA 1g IV Fetch Drug
Post-Procedure Plan
• CT Head/Face.
• Transfer Theatre/Interventional Radiology.
Haemorrhage Control
  1. POSITION: "Lean and Spit" if conscious. Suction constantly. Sweep airway with gloved finger or Magill forceps to clear avulsed teeth and bone fragments.
  2. MIDFACE REDUCTION (Le Fort):
    • "Dish face" appearance.
    • Place gloved fingers behind upper teeth/palate.
    • PULL ANTERIORLY (Hard).
    • Disimpacts the maxilla. Stops bleeding.
    • Insert BITE BLOCK to maintain position.
  3. TONGUE STITCH: Flail mandible? Stitch tongue tip and pull forward.
  4. NASAL PACKING (Choose One):
    Option A: EPISTATS (Double Balloon)
    • Insert. Inflate BOTH balloons at the same time.
    Option B: FOLEYS + RAPID RHINO
    • Insert BOTH FOLEY CATHETERS (one each side) posterior. Inflate posterior balloons with 10 to 15ml of SALINE. Pull back.
    • THEN insert RAPID RHINOS Anteriorly.

LAST (Lipid Rescue)

Page 1: Walkthrough (Preparation)

Recognition
• Call 2222 "Anaesthetic Emergency".
• History: Recent LA injection (Lidocaine/Bupivacaine).
• Prodrome: Tongue tingling, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation.
• CNS: Confusion -> Seizures -> Coma.
• CVS: Hypertension (Early) -> Bradycardia -> Broad QRS -> Asystole.
• Mimic: Anaphylaxis (LAST usually has no rash/wheeze).
Lipid Rescue Kit
Intralipid 20% (CHECK CONCENTRATION) Fetch Drug
Stock: 3-4 bags (1 Litre min)
50ml Syringe + Drawing needle
Wide Bore Cannulae x2
Giving Sets
Benzos (Midazolam) Fetch Drug
📍 STORAGE LOCATION
INTRALIPID IS KEPT IN THE FIRST CUPBOARD ON THE LEFT IN THE MAJORS DRUG CUPBOARD.
Post-Procedure Plan
• ICU Admission.
• Monitor for recurrence (Lipid sink wears off).
Immediate Management
  1. STOP INJECTION: Call for Help & Lipid Kit.
  2. AIRWAY: Intubate. Hyperventilate to pH greater than 7.45 (Acidosis increases free drug).
  3. SEIZURES: Benzos. Small dose Propofol (risk of CVS collapse).
  4. CPR: Extended CPR.
    Note: Avoid Lidocaine. Use Amiodarone. Reduce Adrenaline (less than 1mcg/kg).
  5. LIPID BOLUS: 1.5 ml/kg (approx 100ml for a 70kg adult). Give INTRALIPID 20% over 1 min. Repeat x2 q5min if unstable.
  6. LIPID INFUSION: 15 ml/kg/hr (approx 1000ml/hr for a 70kg adult). Double rate if unstable.

DAS Plan D (Scalpel-Bougie-Tube)
  1. POSITION: Neck Extended. Pillow under shoulders.

  3. INCISION: Transverse stab through CTM. Blade edge TOWARDS YOU.
